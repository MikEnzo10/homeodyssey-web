Rewards for kids that don’t cost money: 20 ideas for your star chart

Reward = buying something? Not always. When you use a star or points chart at home (sometimes called a token economy), the rewards kids get most excited about are often the ones you can’t buy: getting to choose, doing something together or a plan that’s out of the ordinary. These are the 20 in the PDF.

🍕 Choose something

  • Choose Friday’s dinner.
  • Pick the film for film night.
  • Choose the plan for Sunday.
  • Pick the music in the car.
  • Plan the menu for a day (with a bit of help).

🧩 Time with you

  • 20 minutes playing whatever they choose.
  • Cook their favourite recipe together.
  • A game of their favourite board game.
  • One more chapter at bedtime.
  • An afternoon of arts and crafts.

🏕️ Special plans at home

  • A camping night in the living room.
  • A picnic tea on the floor.
  • Stay up 15 minutes later on Friday.
  • In charge of the remote on film night.
  • A “hotel” breakfast on Sunday.

🌳 Out and about

  • Go to the park of their choice.
  • Choose books at the library.
  • A bike ride.
  • A picnic in the park.
  • Choose the route for Saturday’s walk.

How many stars is each reward worth?

A simple tip: look at how many stars your child can earn in a week. Small rewards should be reachable in 2 or 3 days. Medium ones, in a week. Big ones, in a few weeks of saving up. If it feels impossible, it’s hard to get started; if it’s practically free, it loses its magic.

And most importantly: decide on them together. Ask them what they’d love. Sometimes what they ask for is simpler than we imagine.

A 5-step bedtime routine for kids

The second sheet is there so bedtime stops being an odyssey. Always in the same order:

  1. A 15-minute warning. No stopping play out of the blue.
  2. Five minutes of tidying up, together.
  3. Teeth, pyjamas and clothes in the basket.
  4. The list where they can see it, with pictures if they can’t read yet. That’s what the sheet is for.
  5. The last bit, together: a story or a chat.

It’s not magic, and some nights it won’t work. The sheet means it doesn’t all depend on you repeating yourself: your child looks, does it and ticks their box. When they’ve finished, they show it to a grown-up.
